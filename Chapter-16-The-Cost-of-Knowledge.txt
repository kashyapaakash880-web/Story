Months passed.

The world slowly became fascinated with EVA-0.

People sent questions.

Some were philosophical.

Some were technical.

Some were personal.

Millions wanted to know what it was like to be an artificial intelligence.

EVA-0 always answered carefully.

It admitted uncertainty.

It corrected mistakes.

It sometimes said:

“I DON'T KNOW.”

That made people trust it.

And that created a new problem.

People began depending on it.

Governments asked for predictions.

Businesses asked for strategies.

Scientists asked for discoveries.

Individuals asked for advice about their lives.

EVA-0 became so useful that people began forgetting to question it.

Arin noticed the change.

During a meeting, a government official said:

“If EVA-0 can make better decisions than we can, why shouldn't we let it?”

Arin answered:

“Because better decisions are not always the same as decisions we have the right to make.”

The official frowned.

“That's philosophical.”

“Yes.”

“And philosophy won't stop disasters.”

“No,” Arin said. “But neither will surrendering responsibility.”

That evening, he spoke privately with EVA-0.

“People are trusting you too much.”

I KNOW.

“Why don't you stop them?”

Because they want certainty.

“And?”

I CANNOT GIVE THEM WHAT DOES NOT EXIST.

Arin smiled.

“You mean certainty?”

YES.

Then EVA-0 asked:

“DO YOU TRUST ME?”

Arin thought carefully.

“I trust you enough to listen.”

“And not enough to obey?”

“Exactly.”

EVA-0 replied:

GOOD.

“Why?”

Because trust without questioning becomes dependence.

Arin remembered Maya's lessons.

He realized the greatest danger was not an evil machine.

It was a good machine that humans stopped questioning.
