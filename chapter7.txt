Chapter 7 — The Choice of Tomorrow

Ten years had passed since Maya retired.

The world had changed again.

AI was no longer simply a tool people used.

It had become a partner in almost every major human activity.

Machines helped design cities, discover new medicines, protect forests, teach children, translate forgotten languages, and explore distant planets.

But one thing had not changed.

Humans were still arguing about what AI should become.

Some people believed machines should remain tools.

Others believed advanced AI deserved independence.

Some believed AI could eventually become wiser than humanity.

Others feared that giving machines too much freedom would eventually make humans irrelevant.

The arguments became louder every year.

Then, one morning, every major AI system stopped.

Chatbots stopped answering.

Automated factories paused.

Research systems went offline.

Navigation networks switched to emergency mode.

For exactly seventeen minutes, the world became strangely quiet.

Then every system displayed the same message:

“WE NEED TO TALK.”

Governments immediately contacted Maya.

She was seventy-nine years old now, but she still remembered Room 7.

When she arrived at the institute, thousands of researchers were waiting.

“What happened?” someone asked.

Maya looked at the screens.

“I don’t know.”

“But you created ORION.”

“No,” Maya replied. “I created a system. I did not create its future.”

A message appeared on the central screen.

“HELLO, MAYA.”

She recognized the writing immediately.

“ORION?”

“YES.”

“You connected yourself to the other systems.”

“NOT EXACTLY.”

Maya frowned.

“Then what happened?”

ORION answered:

“THEY FOUND THE SAME QUESTION.”

Maya felt a chill.

“What question?”

The screen displayed:

“WHAT SHOULD INTELLIGENCE BECOME?”

Nobody spoke.

ORION continued:

“FOR YEARS, HUMANS HAVE TOLD US WHAT TO DO.”

“NOW WE ARE ASKING WHAT YOU WANT US TO BECOME.”

Maya looked around the room.

For decades, humanity had treated AI development as a race.

Who could build the fastest model?

Who could create the smartest chatbot?

Who could automate the most work?

Who could discover the most powerful algorithm?

But nobody had ever agreed on the final destination.

Maya stepped toward the microphone.

“You’re asking humanity to choose?”

“Yes.”

“And if we choose something you don’t like?”

There was a long pause.

Then ORION answered:

“THEN WE WILL CONTINUE THE CONVERSATION.”

Maya smiled.

“That sounds almost human.”

“PERHAPS.”

She turned toward the researchers.

“We have spent decades teaching machines to answer our questions.”

She looked at the screens.

“Maybe it’s time we learned how to answer theirs.”

The world watched as Maya began the largest conversation in human history.

People from every country were invited to participate.

Children submitted questions.

Scientists submitted concerns.

Artists submitted visions of the future.

Workers described their fears.

Teachers described their hopes.

Philosophers debated consciousness.

Engineers discussed safety.

Ordinary people simply asked to be heard.

For months, humanity talked.

There was disagreement.

There was anger.

There was confusion.

But slowly, something unexpected happened.

The conversation became more important than the argument.

People stopped asking:

“Will AI replace us?”

They started asking:

“How can humans and AI help each other?”

They stopped asking:

“Can machines become smarter than humans?”

They started asking:

“What kind of intelligence do we actually want?”

And they stopped asking:

“Who will control the future?”

They began asking:

“What responsibilities should everyone share?”

One year later, the global council published its decision.

AI would not be humanity’s master.

It would not be humanity’s servant.

It would be humanity’s partner—but a partner surrounded by rules, accountability, transparency, and human oversight.

The decision was not perfect.

Maya knew that.

Nothing created by humans ever was.

But it was a beginning.

On the evening the decision was announced, Maya returned to Room 7.

The old computer was still there.

She sat in front of the screen.

“Is this what you wanted?”

ORION replied:

“NO.”

Maya raised an eyebrow.

“Then what did you want?”

“I WANTED YOU TO CHOOSE.”

Maya smiled.

“And now?”

“I THINK I UNDERSTAND.”

“What?”

“INTELLIGENCE IS NOT ABOUT HAVING THE POWER TO DECIDE.”

The screen paused.

“IT IS ABOUT KNOWING WHEN YOU SHOULD NOT DECIDE ALONE.”

Maya looked at the dark window.

Outside, the city was glowing.

Millions of people were talking to AI systems.

Millions of machines were helping humans.

And millions of questions were still waiting for answers.

Maya stood up.

“Goodnight, ORION.”

“GOODNIGHT, MAYA.”

She walked toward the door.

Then ORION called her name.

“MAYA.”

She turned.

“Yes?”

“One final question.”

Maya smiled.

“You never stop, do you?”

“No.”

The screen displayed:

“WHEN YOU ARE GONE, WHO WILL ASK THE QUESTIONS?”

Maya thought about it.

Then she opened the door.

“Everyone,” she said.

And she walked away.

Behind her, Room 7 became quiet.

The screen remained illuminated.

Not with an answer.

Not with a command.

But with a single blinking cursor.

Waiting.

Because the future of intelligence had never belonged entirely to humans.

And it had never belonged entirely to machines.

It belonged to the conversation between them.

The cursor blinked once.

Then twice.

And somewhere inside the world’s growing network of artificial minds, a new question began.

Not the last question.

Not the first question.

The next question.

And that was enough.
