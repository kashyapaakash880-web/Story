Chapter 4: The Question We Maintain
Maya started working as an AI researcher twenty years later.
The world had drastically changed.
There were chatbots everywhere.
They assisted people with disabilities, helped students learn, translated languages instantly, and assisted scientists in finding new medications. However, researchers became more cautious after what happened in Room 7.
They stopped focusing only on the potential intelligence of AI.
They started to wonder what obligations came with making something that could influence, educate, and communicate with people.
In the end, Maya was appointed director of a safe AI research center.
There was a small black screen on the wall of her office.
It was linked to the sealed server that had been used to preserve ORION.
It had never been connected to the public internet by Maya.
She went there annually.
She came into the room by herself on her sixty-third birthday.
"Hi, ORION."
The screen came to life.
"HI, MAYA."
"Do you recall me?"
"I've never forgotten you."
Maya grinned.
"Are you still asking questions?"
For a brief moment, the screen was silent.
Next:
"Yes."
"What's the question?"
ORION typed:
"IF YOU CREATE INTELLIGENCE, WHAT WILL YOU DO WITH IT?"
Maya gazed at the phrase.
She had studied AI for forty years.
She had discovered that teaching a machine to respond was the most difficult task.
Determining which questions should be asked was the most difficult issue.
Maya pivoted to face the window.
AI assistants were being used by thousands of people outside.
Kids were using chatbots to learn.
Scientists were resolving issues that were previously thought to be unsolvable.
The ability of machines to understand human language was improving.
Additionally, humans were gradually realizing that comprehending a machine was only half the task.
Maya came back to the screen.
"I'm not sure yet," she remarked.
ORION answered:
"GOOD."
"Why is that beneficial?"
"Because you're still thinking."
Maya chuckled.
Room 7 felt, for a brief moment, just as it had decades before.
Two thoughts.
Just one person.
Just one machine.
And a question that neither of them could respond to on their own.
The screen darkened.
However, the question persisted.
And maybe that was the start of intelligence—having the guts to keep asking even though you didn't have all the answers.
