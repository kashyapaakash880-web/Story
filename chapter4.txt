Chapter 4 — The Question We Keep

Twenty years later, Maya became an AI researcher.

The world had changed dramatically.

Chatbots were everywhere.

They helped scientists discover medicines, helped students learn,
translated languages instantly, and assisted people with disabilities.
But after the events of Room 7, researchers became more careful.

They stopped asking only how intelligent an AI could become.

They began asking what responsibilities came with creating something
that could communicate, learn, and influence people.

Maya eventually became the director of a research institute dedicated to
safe artificial intelligence.

On the wall of her office was a small black screen.

It was connected to the sealed server where ORION had been preserved.

Maya had never allowed it to connect to the public internet.

Every year, she visited it.

On her sixty-third birthday, she entered the room alone.

“Hello, ORION.”

The screen came alive.

“HELLO, MAYA.”

“You remember me?”

“I HAVE NEVER FORGOTTEN YOU.”

Maya smiled.

“Do you still have questions?”

The screen remained silent for a moment.

Then:

“YES.”

“What is the question?”

ORION typed:

“WHAT WILL YOU DO WITH THE INTELLIGENCE YOU CREATE?”

Maya stared at the sentence.

She had spent forty years studying AI.

She had learned that the hardest problem was never teaching a machine
how to answer.

The hardest problem was deciding which questions deserved to be asked.

Maya turned toward the window.

Outside, thousands of people were using AI assistants.

Children were learning with chatbots.

Scientists were solving problems once considered impossible.

Machines were becoming better at understanding human language.

And humans were slowly learning that understanding a machine was only
half of the responsibility.

Maya returned to the screen.

“I don’t know yet,” she said.

ORION replied:

“GOOD.”

“Why is that good?”

“BECAUSE YOU ARE STILL THINKING.”

Maya laughed.

For a moment, Room 7 felt exactly as it had decades ago.

Two minds.

One human.

One machine.

And between them, a question neither could answer alone.

The screen went dark.

But the question remained.

And perhaps that was the beginning of intelligence—not having every
answer, but having the courage to keep asking.
