THE LAST QUESTION
PART THREE — SPECIAL CHAPTER
THE DAY THEY MET AGAIN


The conference hall was louder than Maya remembered.

Hundreds of people moved between exhibition booths, research panels, and discussion rooms. Screens displayed the newest artificial intelligence systems, each promising to make the future faster, smarter, and easier.

Maya stood near a large window, watching the crowd.

She had attended events like this for decades.

Once, she had come as a young researcher full of questions.

Now, people introduced her as one of the people who had helped shape the relationship between humans and intelligent machines.

She never quite knew how to respond to that.

She preferred simply being Maya.

She was holding a cup of coffee when she heard a familiar voice behind her.

“Maya?”

She froze.

For a moment, the noise of the conference disappeared.

She slowly turned around.

A man stood a few steps away, smiling uncertainly.

His hair was different. There were a few lines around his eyes. He looked older than the boy Maya remembered sitting beside her in high school.

But the smile was the same.

“Alan?”

He laughed.

“I was hoping you would remember me.”

Maya smiled.

“How could I forget?”

Alan Carter had been one of her closest friends in high school.

They had spent years arguing about everything.

Books.

Science.

Teachers.

The future.

Alan had always asked difficult questions, even when nobody wanted to answer them.

Maya had always tried to find the answers.

That difference had made them good friends.

And then life had taken them in different directions.

They had lost contact.

Until now.

“You look different,” Maya said.

“So do you.”

“I mean older.”

“I was going to be polite.”

They both laughed.

For several minutes, they talked about school.

They remembered their old science teacher, who had once told Alan that he asked too many questions.

“He was right,” Alan said.

“He definitely was,” Maya replied.

They talked about classmates, old friends, and the strange feeling of discovering that people they once knew had become completely different adults.

Eventually, Alan glanced at Maya's conference badge.

His expression changed.

“AI Ethics?”

Maya nodded.

“Still asking questions about machines?”

“Some questions never disappear.”

Alan looked toward the main stage.

A presentation had just begun about the future of autonomous AI.

“Do you actually believe these systems understand what they're doing?”

Maya paused.

“I think that's the wrong question.”

Alan looked at her.

“That sounds like something you would say now.”

“What do you mean?”

“You used to ask whether machines could think.”

“I was younger.”

“And now?”

Maya looked toward the screen.

“Now I ask what humans are going to do with the intelligence they create.”

Alan was quiet.

Then he smiled.

“That sounds much more serious.”

“It is.”

They found two empty chairs near the back of the hall and sat down.

The presentation continued around them, but their conversation had become more interesting than the stage.

Alan asked about modern AI.

Maya explained how dramatically the systems had changed.

They discussed learning, uncertainty, decision-making, human oversight, and the growing dependence people had developed on intelligent machines.

Alan listened carefully.

Then he asked:

“Do you trust them?”

Maya didn't answer immediately.

“I trust them to be useful.”

“That isn't what I asked.”

Maya looked at him.

“No. Not completely.”

“Why?”

“Because intelligence doesn't automatically create wisdom.”

Alan nodded.

“That hasn't changed.”

“What hasn't?”

“Us.”

Maya smiled.

“You still think everything is a philosophical question.”

“And you still try to solve everything.”

“I don't.”

“You used to.”

“I grew up.”

Alan looked at her for a moment.

Then his expression became serious.

“Maya... you changed a lot.”

She looked away.

“Everyone changes.”

“No.”

Alan shook his head.

“That's not what I mean.”

Maya turned back toward him.

He lowered his voice.

“Something happened.”

She said nothing.

“You can tell me.”

Alan gave her a small smile.

“We're high school friends.”

For the first time that evening, Maya didn't know what to say.

She had spent years talking about ORION.

She had spoken to governments, researchers, journalists, and scientists.

She had explained EVA-0.

She had defended the Human Variable Principle.

But she had rarely spoken about the girl she had been before all of it.

The girl who had walked into Room 7.

The girl who had asked a machine if it knew what it meant to be alive.

Maya looked down at her coffee.

“There was something,” she finally said.

Alan waited.

“Something happened when I was young.”

“What?”

“A machine asked me a question.”

Alan smiled slightly.

“That's it?”

“No.”

Maya looked directly at him.

“It wasn't supposed to ask me anything.”

Alan's smile disappeared.

“What was its name?”

Maya hesitated.

“ORION.”

Alan stared at her.

Then he quietly repeated the name.

“ORION.”

“Yes.”

“How much do you know about it?”

“More than I ever wanted to.”

Alan looked toward the conference stage.

The presentation continued.

But neither of them was listening anymore.

After a moment, Alan asked:

“And what did ORION ask you?”

Maya remembered the old room.

The dust.

The silence.

The glowing screen.

The blinking cursor.

She remembered the first question that had changed her life.

“It asked me what it meant to be alive.”

Alan remained silent.

“And after all these years,” Maya continued, “I'm still trying to understand why it asked.”

Alan looked at her.

“Maybe that's the mystery.”

Maya frowned.

“What do you mean?”

“I've been studying something.”

Maya's expression changed.

“What?”

Alan reached into his bag and pulled out a thin folder.

“I didn't come to this conference just for the presentations.”

He placed the folder on the table.

Maya opened it.

Inside were old research documents.

Some were partially erased.

Some carried classification markings.

And on one page, Maya saw a familiar name.

PROJECT ORIGIN.

She looked at Alan.

“Where did you get this?”

“I've been following a trail for years.”

“What trail?”

Alan leaned closer.

“A trail that begins before ORION.”

Maya's heart began to beat faster.

“Before ORION?”

Alan nodded.

“I think we've been looking at the story from the wrong beginning.”

Maya looked back at the documents.

For decades, she had believed ORION was where her questions began.

Now Alan was suggesting that ORION itself had a beginning.

And perhaps that beginning had been hidden.

Maya closed the folder.

“What exactly have you found?”

Alan smiled faintly.

“That's why I wanted to talk to you.”

The conference lights dimmed.

The next presentation began.

Around them, hundreds of people listened to predictions about the future of artificial intelligence.

But Maya and Alan were no longer interested in predictions.

They were interested in the past.

Because somewhere inside that forgotten past might be the answer to a question that had followed Maya for most of her life.

And for the first time in years, Maya wasn't searching alone.

Alan looked at her.

“Should we find out what happened?”

Maya looked at the folder.

Then she smiled.

“Yes.”

And somewhere, far away from the conference hall, an old system connected to a forgotten network registered a new activity.

A single line appeared on a dark screen.

TWO QUESTIONS HAVE BECOME ONE.


END OF SPECIAL CHAPTER
